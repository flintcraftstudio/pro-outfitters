# Old website addresses: where they go after launch

Prepared for Pro Outfitters by Logan / Firefly Software, October 14, 2026.

## What this is

Your current website has been up for years. Google has indexed its pages. Guests have them bookmarked. Old emails, Facebook posts, and other sites link to them.

When the new site goes live, every one of those old addresses will keep working. Each one forwards the visitor to the matching page on the new site. Google treats these forwards as permanent, so the search ranking your old pages earned carries over to the new ones.

You don't need to do anything for this to work. Two decisions below need your answer.

## Two decisions for you

**1. News & Videos.** The new site doesn't have a news section, so that page forwards to the home page for now. If there's a video or story worth keeping, tell me which and I'll give it a home. If not, the forward stays as it is.

**2. Blackfoot River.** It has its own page on the new site for now. If you'd rather fold it into the North Fork Crossing page, I'll point the old address there instead. Either way, nobody hits a dead end.

## What doesn't carry over

A few addresses the old site generated on its own won't forward: the blog's RSS feed, the author page, and some internal archive pages. Nobody visits them and nothing links to them. They'll show the new site's "page not found" page, with links back to the home page and the contact page.

## One thing I'll need at launch

The forwards only work once your domain points to the new site. That's the domain and DNS access on the request list (item 27). I'll also make sure the "www." version of the address and plain "http://" links land on the new site.
